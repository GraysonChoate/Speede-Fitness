# Speede — Brand brief

*Written after capturing the live site (96 URLs, July 2026), recovering the pre-teaser brand site
from the Wayback Machine, and reading the founder interviews and press record. Everything quoted
below is Speede's own language unless marked otherwise.*

---

## 1. The story

**Speede was built by someone the fitness industry failed.**

Dan Mooney was a college pitcher throwing mid-to-upper 90s with a professional career in reach. A
run of injuries ended it. He traveled the country looking for treatment. Nothing worked. He gave up
the game and went home to the Chicago area to train other people instead.

There he trained under John McNulty, a coach who built his work around eccentric movement — the
lowering half of a lift, where muscles are 40–50% stronger and almost every machine ignores them.
Mooney used the method on himself and, in his words, **walked pain-free for the first time in years.**

That's the hinge of the whole company. He didn't discover a market gap in a spreadsheet; he found
the thing that fixed his body and then discovered nobody had made it safe or affordable for anyone
who wasn't in a lab or a pro facility:

> "I realized that no one was offering a technology-driven platform that offered full-body
> resistance training. And, more importantly, no one was offering a safe, cost-effective way to
> perform eccentric training." — Dan Mooney

In September 2020 he partnered with **Greg Tepas**, a career operator who became CEO. They took the
build to aerospace engineers (Hatch Product Development) and motion-capture specialists rather than
to a fitness-equipment factory. The site's own version of this, verbatim, is the best short telling
of it — **and it should survive any redesign intact**:

> ### The Story of Speede
> As he worked towards a career as a professional baseball player, Dan Mooney's playing days were
> cut short by a devastating injury. After trying to make a recovery, he realized that his true
> path was to help other athletes build strength and avoid injuries like his. Dan knew the value of
> eccentric movements for rapidly building strength, but saw that these movements were not safe or
> accessible for the average athlete. Together with fitness mentors, technology experts, and
> co-founder Greg Tepas—a visionary business leader—Dan and the team developed this idea into an
> all-in-one strength training solution called Speede.
>
> Now, as Speede enters the market, Dan and Greg share a vision of equipping every person—regardless
> of their current fitness level—with the tools and inspiration they need to reach their full
> potential.

Even the name carries the thesis. From their own FAQ:

> "It's pronounced like 'speed.' (The last 'e' is silent.) Our name was inspired by how quickly our
> machine is able to create momentary muscular failure (MMF), which is the key to building strength
> faster."

**Beneath the surface copy, this is a company about honest work.** Not "workout smarter." Not
life-hacking. The claim is that most gym time is *wasted* — spent guessing at weights, fighting the
sticking point, doing fifteen sloppy reps because the machine can't tell the difference. Speede's
promise is that if you remove the guessing, five real reps beat fifteen fake ones. The time savings
are a *consequence* of rigor, not a substitute for it. That distinction is the brand's spine.

**Where they are now.** Speede raised an oversubscribed $2.5M seed in 2022 and signed NFL, NBA and
UFC athletes as *equity holders* — not endorsers. In May 2024, Hydrow acquired a majority stake. The
live site today is a single-page "coming soon" teaser for a relaunch: a smaller, home-scale machine
that **folds flat to slide under your bed**, with **500 lbs in React Mode**. The earlier commercial
machine claimed up to 2,000 lbs. This is a deliberate move from the pro facility to the living room
— and the teaser's tagline says it plainly: *"Pro-grade force. Anyone's living room."*

## 2. Who they serve, and the transformation

**The stated audience, in their words:** *"Speede is designed for athletes, but made for everyone."*

That line is doing more work than it looks. It isn't hedging — it's a hierarchy. Speede builds to
the standard of the most demanding user, then hands the same machine to everyone else without
watering it down. The teaser makes this concrete with four faces:

| | Who | What they came for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The athlete** | An edge. Off-season gains, in-season maintenance without the fatigue cost |
| 2 | **The comeback** | Rehab and reconditioning — the founder's own story, sold back |
| 3 | **The 60-year PR** | Strength as longevity; still setting records |
| 4 | **The first-timer** | No idea what weight to pick — and now doesn't have to |

Historically the business also served coaches, physical and occupational therapists, colleges, high
schools, and pro teams (Speede Pro, the commercial unit). The relaunch is consumer-first.

**The transformation:** you stop guessing. Today, walking into a gym means choosing a number — and
that number is wrong for most of the movement, too heavy where you're weak and too light where
you're strong. Speede removes the choice. The machine reads your force and matches it, inch by inch.
What you get back is **certainty and time**: proof you gave maximum effort, and your evening returned
to you.

Their sharpest framing of it is still the current homepage line:

> **"Five honest reps do what fifteen sloppy ones can't."**

## 3. What's genuinely unique

Ranked by how hard each would be for a competitor to copy.

1. **The mechanism is real, and it's rare.** Isokinetic resistance (constant speed, resistance
   varying to match your force) plus supramaximal eccentric overload, in one connected machine.
   Tonal and Speediance do digital weight; they don't do true isokinetic. This is engineering, not
   positioning — you can't launch it with a rebrand.
2. **Athletes backed it with money, not just a signature.** Speede's **2022** press release names
   Justin Fields, Cole Kmet, Jaylon Johnson, Justin Simmons, Marquez Valdes-Scantling and Mason
   Plumlee as *equity holders*, and lists Michael Chandler as Chief Athletic Officer. ⚠️ **Treat as
   historical.** This is pre-Hydrow; equity and titles routinely change in an acquisition and none
   of it has been confirmed since. On-page language should be **"backed by"**, not "owns equity in",
   unless Speede confirms current status.
3. **The founder's origin is unrepeatable.** A career ended by injury, then fixed by the method the
   company now sells. This is a story asset, not evidence — it explains *why they built it*, and it
   should never be asked to carry the *does it work* argument. That job belongs to the athlete
   quotes, the force numbers, and the bibliography.
4. **They publish their sources.** The science page carries a full academic bibliography — Schoenfeld,
   Fisher & Steele, Hedayatpour, Milner-Brown. That's a stronger science trail than most connected
   strength pages carry, and it converts a marketing claim into a checkable one.
5. **The disbelief reaction is consistent and specific.** Read the testimonials together and they're
   all the same *shape* — not "I love it," but *"I didn't think that was possible."*
   - *"It was kind of just baffling."* — Michael Chandler
   - *"It was like I just did a 45-minute bicep workout in five reps."* — Justin Simmons
   - *"Within five or 10 minutes on Speede, I feel like I've done a 30-minute workout."* — Justin Fields
   - *"First rep on this, I actually said 'what just happened?' out loud."* — anonymous, current teaser

   **This is the single most valuable asset the brand owns and the site currently buries it.**
   Full verbatim set in Appendix A.
6. **It disappears.** Pro-grade force that folds flat and slides under a bed. Tonal needs a wall.
   A rack needs a room. This needs a mat.
7. **They name the number.** *"Most machines hide their number — or cap out low."* Publishing a
   direct comparison table against Tonal and AMP is a posture competitors can't take without losing.

## 4. Voice and tone

**Blunt · Credentialed · Warm · Unimpressed by hype**

Speede talks like a strength coach who's seen everything, respects you enough to be direct, and has
the citations if you want them. It never oversells, because its whole argument is that everyone else
does.

- **Blunt.** Short declaratives. Periods used as punches.
  > *"The most effective strength training, period."* · *"Stronger starts soon."* ·
  > *"You'll never go back to free weights again."* · *"Strength training will never be the same."*
- **Credentialed.** It will use the real word and then explain it, rather than dodge it.
  > *"That's isokinetic + eccentric overload: more real work in fewer reps, without a rack of plates
  > or a guess about the weight."*
- **Warm.** In long form it drops the swagger entirely and gets on the reader's side.
  > *"We've all heard trainers, fitness influencers, and gym rats give the same tired 'what's your
  > excuse?' speech before, right? … the truth is, a lot of us are just really busy. We have families,
  > full time jobs, and a packed schedule. There actually ARE a lot of genuine reasons why someone
  > might have less time … and that's okay."*
- **Unimpressed by hype.** It positions against the category, including its own category.
  > *"This isn't another tech company with no knowledge of health, fitness & performance looking to
  > capitalize."* · *"Most machines hide their number — or cap out low. Ours is clean, ownable, and
  > heavier. Here's the honest board:"*

**Tonal rules to hold to:** never exclamation marks; never "crush it," "grind," "beast mode," or any
of the standard fitness-bro register; numbers are stated flatly and never inflated; the reader is
never told they're lazy.

## 5. What's already working — preserve this

**Copy that should not be rewritten.** These lines are the brand:
- *"Stronger starts soon."* / *"You've never seen this."*
- *"Five honest reps do what fifteen sloppy ones can't."*
- *"Pro-grade force. Anyone's living room."*
- *"Designed for athletes, but made for everyone."*
- *"Most machines hide their number — or cap out low."*
- *"The whole gym. One mat."*
- *"We're not showing you everything. Not yet."*
- *"The most efficient strength training, period."*
- The entire *Story of Speede* paragraph, and the pronunciation FAQ.

**Structure that already flows.** The teaser's narrative order is genuinely well-built and should
survive: hook → **one ownable number** → the science in plain language → a withheld glimpse of the
product → who it's for → proof → CTA. Very few teaser pages have this much spine. Keep the order.

**Design decisions worth keeping.**
- The palette. `#080a09` near-black with a green bias, `#b2ff59` lime, `#f4f7f0` warm-white,
  `#9aa39a` muted. Every "neutral" is nudged toward green — that's why it doesn't read as a stock
  dark theme. Keep the exact hexes.
- **Space Grotesk + Inter.** Geometric-but-quirky display against a neutral workhorse. Correct
  pairing for a company that's technical without being cold.
- The signature typographic move: a **220px numeral next to a 10px wide-tracked uppercase label**.
  That extreme scale contrast is the brand's visual accent.
- The `500` numeral gradient-clipped from white into lime.
- The **honest board** — the comparison table naming Tonal and AMP. Bold and on-message.
- The **force-curve graph** (38 lbs concentric / 59 lbs eccentric). A real data visualization
  explaining a real mechanism. Rare and valuable — keep it and make it better.
- The photography. Real machine, real rooms, hard daylight on concrete and wood. Not a white void,
  not a gym-lit set. Grounded and expensive-looking.

## 6. What's holding it back from feeling premium

Diagnosis in priority order — these become the Phase 3 brief.

1. **The proof is a footnote.** Six named professional athletes with equity in the company, and the
   live page shows *one anonymous quote* — *"— Pro athlete, first session"* — under four plain
   league wordmarks. This is the most credible thing Speede has and it's rendered as the smallest
   thing on the page. It should be a movement of its own.
2. **Vertical rhythm is flat.** Every section is `padding: 90px 0`. Nothing breathes differently,
   so nothing feels more important than anything else. Premium comes from *variation* in space —
   the hero and the proof section should own far more of it than the render grid does.
3. **The hero splits its own punch.** *"Stronger starts soon."* and *"You've never seen this."* are
   two separate hooks stacked in one H1, with the second in muted grey — which reads as an
   afterthought rather than a build. One is the promise; the other is the tease. They need different
   weights and different jobs.
4. **The tease section doesn't tease.** *"We're not showing you everything. Not yet."* is followed
   immediately by three clear, fully-lit product photographs. The copy promises restraint; the layout
   delivers a product gallery. The visual language has to actually withhold — crop, shadow,
   partial reveal — or the line is just a line.
5. **The science is over in one paragraph.** *"5 reps = 38"* is a strong idea buried in a two-column
   block with a small chart. The bibliography, the strength-curve explanation, the fast-twitch
   recruitment argument — none of it is reachable. The one thing no competitor can copy is
   compressed into 60 words.
6. **The founder story is entirely absent.** The most human, most defensible asset — a pitcher whose
   career ended, who fixed himself and built the machine — appears nowhere on the live site. The
   `/pages/about` URL still resolves and renders an empty shell.
7. **CTA language is generic and inconsistent.** *"Get early access"* / *"Notify me"* / *"Join the
   waitlist"* — three labels for one action, none of them in Speede's voice. The supporting
   micro-copy is standard SaaS reassurance (*"No spam. Unsubscribe anytime."*) from a brand whose
   whole tone is that it doesn't need to reassure you.
8. **Nothing moves on scroll.** Two decorative animations exist (a pulsing dot, a marquee) and no
   entrance motion at all — so arrival at each section is inert, and the marquee is the busiest
   thing on the page. Backwards. Also: no `prefers-reduced-motion` handling.
9. **Typography lacks a middle register.** It jumps from 220px to 15px with very little in between,
   so long-form copy has nowhere to live and everything that isn't a headline reads as small print.
10. **Accessibility gaps.** Four images ship with empty `alt`; muted body text at `#9aa39a` on
    `#0e120f` sits near the AA floor; the nav collapses to nothing below 860px (links simply
    `display:none` with no replacement); there is a single breakpoint for the entire page.
11. **It's a Shopify page carrying 60+ third-party scripts.** Affirm, TalkShopLive, Facebook Pixel,
    GTM, a GDPR widget, and the full theme bundle load to render what is functionally one static
    page with one email field. Speed *is the brand name*.

---

## 7. Recommendation for Phase 3

**Tech: static HTML/CSS/JS** — no framework.

The deliverable is one narrative page with one form. There is no routing, no data layer, and no
application state. React or Next would add a build step and a hydration cost to a page whose entire
argument is efficiency. Static also means the result can be dropped straight back into the existing
Shopify custom-liquid block the current teaser already lives in, with no platform migration. If the
full product site returns later (product, science, athletes, story), the same tokens and components
scale to multiple static pages without rework.

**The through-line for the rebuild:** *the moment of disbelief.* Every real piece of proof this brand
owns is someone saying they didn't think it was possible. Build the page so it earns that reaction
and then hands the reader six people who already had it.

## Appendix 0 — ⚠️ Do not use: content belonging to the discontinued machine

**Added after the 7/28 huddle with Simon Stein.** Speede replaced its hardware. The Wayback
recovery in `/original/archive` is a mix of *live brand truth* and *dead product*, and the two look
identical in a text file. Anything below describes the **old Speede Challenger** — a large
cable-rack unit with a 32" screen that the company has moved on from. Putting it on the page would
pitch a redesign of a product they killed.

| ❌ Do not use | ✅ Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Up to 2,000 lbs of resistance" | **500 lbs · React Mode** |
| Mode names **Nemesis** / **Excentric™** | **React Mode** / **Standard Mode** |
| $5,995 + $29.99–39.99/mo, pre-order, 30-day trial | No pricing. The CTA is the waitlist. |
| 32" HD touchscreen, two AI cameras, two 50W speakers, 10+ sensors | The compact unit: screen on an arm, mat base, folds flat |
| "3 × 5 ft footprint", Speede Pro / commercial framing | "The whole gym. One mat." · consumer-first |
| Old photography — big cable rack, treadmill-sized (all YouTube results) | Only the 8 JPEGs on the current site |

**Still true and safe:** the founder story, the science (isokinetic + eccentric overload, strength
curve, fast-twitch recruitment, the bibliography), the athlete roster and their equity stake,
Michael Chandler as Chief Athletic Officer, "designed for athletes, made for everyone", the
pronunciation story, and the brand voice.

**Two things found in the current site's own assets:**

1. **`9-image.jpg` shows a screen with the `hydrow` wordmark on the bezel and Hydrow-blue UI.**
   Hydrow took a majority stake in May 2024, and this image shows that relationship reaching the
   product. ⚠️ **It does not establish who builds the hardware** — a bezel mark is not a
   manufacturing claim, and an earlier draft of this brief overstated it. Speede publishes the image
   themselves so it is safe to use, but do not scale it to hero size, where the parent-company mark
   reads as a competitor's logo to anyone who doesn't know the deal.
2. **That same screen reads `38.6 LB/CABLE — 5/6`.** The teaser's best line, *"5 reps = 38"*, is a
   literal transcription of the machine's own readout. The force-curve section should therefore be
   designed as an instrument readout, because that is exactly what it is.
